Where the sky actually goes dark

Winter (Oct to Mar) usually means clearer skies. Check road and weather conditions before heading to the mountains.

Hanle, Ladakh

India's first Dark Sky Reserve, at around 4,500 m. Home to an astronomical observatory. Pack serious warm layers.

Kibber, Spiti

A high-altitude village in Himachal with almost no light pollution and a huge open horizon.

Rann of Kutch, Gujarat

Flat white salt desert, so you get sky from edge to edge. Easy to combine with a winter trip.

Thar, Rajasthan

Dunes near Jaisalmer are far from big cities and dry, so skies are often crisp.

First time? Start here

  • Get 20 km from city lights. Even a village rooftop beats a metro balcony.
  • Give your eyes 20 minutes. Dim your phone or use night mode, bright screens reset your night vision.
  • Skip the telescope. Your eyes plus a free app like Stellarium Web is plenty. Binoculars are a bonus.
  • For meteors, lie flat. Look at the wide sky, not one spot, and bring a mat and a flask of chai.
